How the adjustments are calibrated

Why a feature is worth what it is worth here

A bathroom in a million-dollar home is worth far more than the same bathroom in a $50,000 home. Adjustments only make sense when they are scaled to the price point of the property. Everything in this analysis is calibrated to the JHT $250K–$500K price band, which covers this Sea Colony non-waterfront segment.

Calibrated to this market

  • Half bath: $3,000 (31 Andover Dr has one more than the subject; this is the only feature adjustment needed across all 4 comps, since every comp otherwise matches the subject's beds, full baths, garage, and pool exactly).
  • Pool + cage: $20,000 of contributory value in this band. No kept comp has a private pool, so this line is informational only; two Sea Colony sales with private pools were screened out rather than adjusted around.
  • Garage bay: $10,000. All 4 comps match the subject's 2-car garage, so no garage adjustment was needed.
  • Renovation: not a line item in the price-band table, but the biggest real driver of spread in this comp set. 58 Bristol Ln's 2024–2025 full remodel (roof, HVAC, water heater, kitchen, flooring) plausibly explains its $344.43/sq ft close versus $182.31–$218.00/sq ft for the other two solds.

Why this matters for your list price

Because 3 of the 4 kept comps match the subject almost feature-for-feature, this analysis leans more heavily on the raw $/sq ft comparison than on line-item adjustments. The subject's own roof, HVAC, and water heater condition are undocumented (no permits on file), so where it lands inside the $316,000–$346,000 range should be confirmed once condition is verified: closer to the top if recently updated, closer to the bottom if original-age systems remain.

Methodology, confidence and sources

How this was built

Method

  • Subject facts pulled from the Flagler County Property Appraiser record (qPublic), the Flagler County Tax Collector, the Flagler Clerk of Court's recorded Warranty Deed, RPR, and the county GIS map viewer, then cross-checked against Zillow.
  • Comps drawn from Stellar MLS, searched inside Sea Colony first (per the JHT community-first search order) and screened for beds, pool, garage, and water frontage against the satellite map rather than the MLS Waterfront flag alone. A HUD distressed sale and two private-pool sales were reviewed and excluded rather than silently dropped.
  • Value derived three ways: the Sea Colony matched-set price per heated square foot, an adjusted sales-comparison grid using the JHT $250K–$500K price-band table, and reconciliation against all seven AVMs.

Data confidence

  • High on subject facts and all 4 matched comp sales (all MLS/county-recorded, closing prices verified).
  • Genuine gap: no building permits are on file with the City of Palm Coast for this address, so roof, HVAC, and water heater age/condition are unconfirmed; iMapp (property report and 1-mile demographics) did not load under this session's Stellar SSO launch and is not included, though its property-level facts duplicate the appraiser record already captured. A GIS aerial screenshot could not be saved to file, though the no-canal finding was confirmed on-screen.
  • All three MLS systems were searched with the same address, because agents outside the local board sometimes list a home in the "wrong" MLS. Stellar MLS is primary for Flagler County and is the basis for all 4 kept comps. realMLS (Flexmls) was searched via its FBS data-share (which also carries Hernando, St. Augustine/St. Johns, Daytona Beach, Space Coast, and Southeast MLS Alliance); it returned only historical Sea Colony closed sales from 2006, 2018, and 2022–2023 (outside the comp window) and one bed-mismatched active-under-contract listing; 0 usable current matches, a real result, not a skipped step. Citrus County MLS was searched and returned 0 matches, also recorded rather than assumed.
