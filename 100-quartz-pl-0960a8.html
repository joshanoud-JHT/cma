How these are prioritized

A pool and the number of garage bays are the two hardest features to price, so they are treated as match-first criteria rather than something to adjust around. This home has no pool and a 2-car garage, so the set is screened to no-pool homes only. Beyond that, the ranking driver here is stories: this is a one-story home and only one comparable sale shares that layout, so it carries the most weight.

Best match = no pool, 2-car, one story. No-pool = matches on pool, differs on layout or garage. Context = usable but further from the subject on size or bed count.

How the adjustments are calibrated

Why a feature is worth what it is worth here

A bathroom in a million-dollar home is worth far more than the same bathroom in a $150,000 home. Adjustments only make sense scaled to the price point. Everything here is calibrated to the JHT $450K–$650K band, selected from the comp evidence.

Calibrated to this market

  • Living area: about $90/sq ft for differences, well below the $182 average sale price per foot, because the marginal value of extra space is always less than the average.
  • Bedroom: $6,000 per bedroom in this band. This home has 3 on the county record while most comps have 4, so those comps are adjusted down.
  • Half bath: $3,500, the band value derived from the full-bath figure.
  • One story: $15,000 credited to two-story comps. This is the judgment call in the model and the slider is there so you can test it. Set it to zero and the reconciled value drops to about $450,000; the case for the higher figure rests on the one-story sale at $206/sq ft and on 31 Olivine Ct asking $465,000 for a smaller one-story house.

The lesson is not that it is worth less than the comps say; it is that pricing above the evidence in a 4-month-inventory market produces days on market, not offers. The adjustments above keep the comparison fair, and the one-story premium is credited openly rather than buried, so the recommended price reflects what a buyer will actually pay for this specific house.

Methodology, confidence and sources

How this was built

Method

  • Subject facts pulled from the St. Johns County Property Appraiser record and cross-checked against Realist tax data. County heated area governs every price-per-foot figure in this report.
  • Comps searched in all three MLS systems with the same criteria, then combined and de-duplicated by address, because the same home often appears on two boards with different data.
  • Every comp verified individually at the county appraiser for heated area, bedrooms, baths, garage size, lot size, year built and sale price. That check caught two pools the county extra-features record had missed and one 30% square-footage discrepancy between MLS feeds.
  • Seller concessions pulled from the MLS listing detail and deducted before any price-per-foot calculation.
  • Value derived three ways and reconciled: the adjusted sales-comparison grid, the price-per-foot picture across the community, and the seven AVMs.

Data confidence

  • High on subject facts, on all seven comp sale prices, and on heated area for every comp (county-recorded).
  • High on concessions for six of seven comps. 52 Opal Way lists concessions as "call listing agent", so it is treated as zero, which is conservative-high on that comp.
  • Medium on the $15,000 one-story premium. It is a judgment figure supported by one closed sale and one active listing, and the slider in the adjustment section lets you test it.
  • Needs confirming: bedroom count. County says 3, MLS and Zillow say 4. This report uses 3.
  • All seven AVMs were retrieved. Realist RealAVM carries a confidence score of 96.

Gaps in this report

  • No interior photos, permit history or renovation detail were available, so condition is assumed average to good for a 2016 home. If the house has been updated, the recommendation can move up.
  • The nine-document source package (appraiser record, GIS map, permit search, tax bill, deed) was not saved as PDFs this run; the underlying records were read live and are cited below.
  • No comp photos are embedded in this edition.
