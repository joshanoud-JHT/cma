How the adjustments are calibrated

Why a feature is worth what it is worth here

A bathroom in a million-dollar home is worth far more than the same bathroom in a $150,000 home. Adjustments only make sense when they are scaled to the price point. Everything in this analysis is calibrated to the JHT $250K to $500K band, selected from the team's 26-band table because the subject's estimated value falls inside it.

Band values applied

  • Bedroom: $5,000. Not used here, every comp is 4-bed like the subject.
  • Full bath: $9,000. Not used here, every comp is 2 full baths.
  • Half bath: $3,000, derived from the full-bath value. This is the one feature adjustment that applies: the subject has a half bath and none of the seven comps do.
  • Garage bay: $10,000. Not used here, every comp is 2-car. Worth noting the subject's garage is 557 sq ft against 380 to 440 sq ft on the comps, so it is an oversized 2-car and that is a showing advantage rather than a line-item adjustment.
  • Private pool: $20,000. Not used, no pool on either side.

Why this matters for your list price

Because the hard features all match, this analysis rests almost entirely on price per square foot and on condition. That is a good position to be in: the arithmetic is clean and the argument is easy for a buyer's agent and an appraiser to follow. It also means condition carries the whole spread. A 1997 home with a 2024 A/C, 2020 windows and an original 1997 kitchen is genuinely mid-band, and the two levers that move it up are the roof and the interior.

Buyers in this band are payment-sensitive and shop on price per foot. Over-improving before listing rarely returns dollar for dollar. Getting ahead of the roof usually does, because it removes a financing and insurance obstacle rather than adding a nice-to-have.

Methodology, confidence and sources

How this was built

Method

  • Subject facts pulled from the Flagler County Property Appraiser record and cross-checked against the Florida Department of Revenue parcel roll, which agreed on heated area, lot size and year built.
  • Comps held to the subject's profile: 4 bed / 2 full bath single-family, 1,949 to 2,149 heated sq ft, no pool, 2-car garage, built 1987 to 2007, lot under half an acre, no HOA. Every candidate then verified against its own county record.
  • Canal, HOA and barrier-island screening done on the satellite map rather than by trusting the MLS waterfront flag.   • Value derived three ways and reconciled: the matched-set adjusted price per heated square foot, the JHT adjustment grid at the $250K to $500K band, and a cross-check against the AVM panel.
  • Recommended price rounded to a clean transitional price point per team rule, and the seller-net figures use standard Florida costs.

  • High on subject facts. Two independent county-level sources agree on 2,049 heated sq ft, 11,875 sq ft lot and 1997 construction, and the permit history is complete and all-final.
  • High on the seven kept sales. All are county-verified for heated area and pool status.
